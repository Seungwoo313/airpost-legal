3. Collection Methods and Legal Grounds

1. Information is obtained from User input and transmission, external sign-in providers, records generated by use and support communications. 2. In Korea, processing necessary for account management and the requested Service relies on contract formation or performance under Article 15(1)(4) of the Personal Information Protection Act. Optional processing requiring consent relies on consent under Article 15(1)(1). 3. Safety, security and protection of rights may rely on Article 15(1)(6) only following an assessment of the legitimate purpose, necessity and balance with User rights. Legal-obligation processing is limited to obligations that actually apply. 4. Operating-system permissions for location, microphone and notifications are distinct from legally required consent. A device permission does not replace separate consent where required. 5. Refusing necessary processing may prevent the relevant Service from being provided. Refusal of an optional feature’s information does not restrict unrelated features.

2. When account deletion is accepted, new use is restricted while external sign-in disconnection, authored content and attachment deletion, and authentication account and profile removal proceed. Provider processing or failures may require retries, so acceptance and completion are separate stages. 3. Correspondents’ own letters are handled as their content. Minimal records of designs already acquired by other Members may remain without providing the departing Member’s text, audio or drawings. Any physical copies are securely destroyed, for example by shredding. Restoring backups requires reapplying previously completed deletion requests. 5. Device drafts and downloaded attachment caches are cleared on sign-out or withdrawal. Revoking a device permission or uninstalling the app does not itself delete server accounts or content. 6. Deleting information within the Service does not recall copies saved or forwarded outside it. Suspected rights infringements may be reported to support. 7. Minimum records needed for ongoing reports, disputes or active sanctions are separately managed for a specific purpose and lawful basis, with periodic review of necessity. Once that reason ends, the rules above apply. This is not a general basis for indefinite retention. Separate statutory preservation duties follow the relevant law and period.

3. Users may request transfer information and exercise applicable rights through support. Refusal of optional transfers may affect only the relevant feature; refusal of a transfer necessary for the core Service may prevent its provision. Specific effects will be explained in the relevant notice or consent process. ## 8. Location and Device Permissions

1. Permitted device location is used to suggest airports and identify cities. The app does not store raw GPS coordinates in the Airpost account database; it stores the selected airport and related city. Apple services support maps and city lookup. 2. The microphone is used when the User records audio. Recordings are created locally and selected files are uploaded during letter sending. Unsent uploads left after a failure follow Section 4. 3. Notification permission enables service alerts. Permissions can be changed in device settings; this does not automatically delete content stored on servers.

9. Cookies, Automatic Collection and Tracking

1. The Airpost app does not use advertising or cross-app or cross-site advertising tracking SDKs. It does not sell or share personal information for targeted advertising. 2. Local session storage and necessary operational records are handled separately from advertising tracking. The Operator does not add separate advertising or analytics scripts to the policy website. 3. External sign-in pages and hosting providers may use their own cookies or technical records under their policies. Browser cookie restrictions may affect external sign-in and similar functions. 4. There is currently no advertising sale or sharing activity to opt out of. Before introducing it, the Operator must implement required consent, opt-out mechanisms and browser-signal handling.

10. Safeguards

1. The Operator applies encrypted communications, account-based authorization, database and attachment access restrictions, authentication protection and necessary access management. 2. Operational and report-review access is limited to necessary information and purposes. The Operator works to keep unnecessary letter text and authentication secrets out of logs. 3. Private letters are not end-to-end encrypted. Authorized operators and processors may handle information as needed for service delivery or lawful safety review. 4. For a personal information incident, the Operator investigates, takes protective action and provides User and regulator notifications required by applicable law.

11. Rights and Requests

1. Users may request access, correction, deletion, suspension of processing, withdrawal of consent and applicable transmission or portability rights under the relevant law. 2. Requests can be submitted through account management functions or to forwoobusiness@gmail.com. Incomplete registration, restrictions or inability to sign in do not prevent email requests. 3. Representatives’ requests are verified using the minimum information needed to establish identity and authority. Excessive identity information relative to the request is not required. 4. Requests are handled within applicable statutory time limits and procedures. Restrictions, refusals or extensions will be accompanied by the permitted basis, reasons and available remedies. 5. Other Members’ rights, legal preservation obligations or other lawful exceptions may limit particular requests. Users will not be unfairly discriminated against for exercising rights.

12. Age and Sensitive Information

1. The Service is intended for people aged 16 or older and does not permit underage registration. Suspected underage use is assessed using the minimum necessary information and followed by appropriate restriction and deletion measures. 2. Government identification numbers and identity-document copies are not ordinary registration fields. Audio and drawings are not analyzed as biometric identifiers. 3. Content entered by Members may include health, religious, sexual or other sensitive information. Some jurisdictions also classify private correspondence or precise location as sensitive. The Operator therefore does not represent all Service information as non-sensitive. 4. Processing that requires additional conditions, including separate consent, must satisfy them. The Operator does not request unnecessary sensitive information and accepts exposure or deletion inquiries.

13. Automated Decisions and Additional Uses

1. Automation may support discovery, delivery calculations and abuse prevention. The current Service does not operate solely automated decision-making that produces legal or similarly significant effects on Users. 2. The Operator does not use Member Content for advertising profiling or general-purpose AI model training. 3. New purposes or automated decisions require appropriate grounds, notice, consent, explanations and objection procedures before introduction.

14. Additional Regional Rights

1. This Section applies where the relevant jurisdiction’s legal conditions are met. It does not reduce any mandatory rights. 2. United States: applicable state laws may grant rights to know and obtain copies, correct or delete, opt out of sale, sharing, targeted advertising or certain profiling, limit sensitive information uses, use authorized agents and appeal refusals. Applicability depends on residence and statutory business thresholds. Categories, sources, purposes and recipients are set out in Sections 2–7. Canada, Australia, Brazil and other regions: the Operator honors applicable information, access, correction, deletion, consent withdrawal and regulatory complaint rights.
